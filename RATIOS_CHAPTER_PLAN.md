# Ratios Chapter - Development Plan

## Learning Objectives (First Principles)

### What Students MUST Understand:

1. **Ratio = Comparison**
   - NOT: "A ratio is two numbers with a colon"
   - BUT: "A ratio compares quantities - how much of one thing compared to another"
   - Foundation: Counting and grouping

2. **Ratios Show Relationships**
   - NOT: "8:12 is a ratio"
   - BUT: "For every 8 strawberries, we use 12 raspberries"
   - The relationship stays the same when we scale up/down

3. **Equivalent Ratios**
   - NOT: "Multiply both sides by the same number"
   - BUT: "If we double the recipe, we double EVERYTHING"
   - Like fractions: 1/2 = 2/4 = 3/6

4. **Simplifying Ratios**
   - NOT: "Divide by GCF"
   - BUT: "Find the smallest whole numbers that keep the same relationship"
   - Like reducing fractions to simplest form

## Pedagogical Approach

### First Principles Teaching:

1. **Start Concrete**
   - Physical objects (strawberries, raspberries)
   - Countable, visible
   - Build intuition

2. **Move to Visual**
   - Diagrams showing groupings
   - Tables showing patterns
   - Graphs showing relationships

3. **Then Abstract**
   - Introduce notation (8:12)
   - Teach operations (simplify, scale)
   - Apply to word problems

4. **Always Connect Back**
   - "What does 2:3 MEAN?"
   - "For every 2 strawberries, 3 raspberries"
   - Never lose the concrete meaning

### Common Misconceptions to Address:

❌ "Ratios are just division"
✅ "Ratios show relationships that stay constant"

❌ "8:12 and 12:8 are the same"
✅ "ORDER MATTERS! 8:12 means 8 of first, 12 of second"

❌ "I can add the same number to both sides"
✅ "NO! You must MULTIPLY both sides by the same number"

❌ "Ratios are always less than 1"
✅ "Ratios can be any size - 5:2 means more of the first thing"

### Problem-Solving Strategy:

**TEACH THIS PROCESS:**

1. **Understand**: What are we comparing?
2. **Identify**: What's the ratio?
3. **Simplify**: Can we make it simpler?
4. **Scale**: Do we need more or less?
5. **Check**: Does our answer make sense?

Student should be able to:
- [ ] Explain what a ratio means in words
- [ ] Write ratios three ways (8:12, 8 to 12, 8/12)
- [ ] Identify equivalent ratios
- [ ] Simplify ratios to simplest form
- [ ] Scale ratios up and down
- [ ] Solve word problems using ratios
- [ ] Explain WHY their answer makes sense
