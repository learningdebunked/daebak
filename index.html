Finding Area — 6th Grade

California Common Core · All 4 area formulas with diagrams, real-world connections, and 10 practice problems.

💡 Learn WHY formulas work → 🌍 See real-world examples →

□ Rectangle

A = l × w
✓ Multiply the two different side lengths.
🏠 At home: Door · TV screen · Phone · Notebook · Window · Table top

▱ Parallelogram

A = b × h
⚠ h = dotted perpendicular line — NOT the slanted side!
🏠 At home: Ramp side-view · Diamond tiles · Staircase railing · Like a rectangle pushed sideways

△ Triangle

A = ½ × b × h
✓ Triangle = half a rectangle. Always divide by 2!
🏠 At home: Pizza slice · Sandwich cut diagonally · Tortilla chip · Home roofline · Yield sign

⭂ Trapezoid

A = ½ × (b₁+b₂) × h
✓ Add BOTH bases first, then × height, then ÷ 2
🏠 At home: Bucket/pail · Lampshade · Bathtub end view · Paper cup opened flat

⚠ Top Mistakes to Avoid on the Test

❌Parallelogram & Triangle: The slanted edge is NOT the height. Use the dotted perpendicular line with the right-angle square □ — that's h.
❌Triangle: Every triangle = half a rectangle. You must divide by 2 — always.
❌Trapezoid: Add b₁ + b₂ first, then multiply by h. Don't skip the addition step!
❌Units: Always write square units — cm², ft², in², m². Missing units = lost points.
✓The little square □ in a diagram marks a 90° right angle — that's always where h meets the base.

🧩 Complex / Composite Shapes

🔑 THE RULE: Break the big shape into simple shapes → Find each area → ADD or SUBTRACT

➕ ADD — "House Shape"

  • Rectangle: 10 × 5 = 50 cm²
  • Triangle: ½ × 10 × 4 = 20 cm²
  • Total: 50 + 20 = 70 cm²

➕ ADD — "L-Shape"

  • Area A: 3 × 8 = 24 cm²
  • Area B: 7 × 4 = 28 cm²
  • Total: 24 + 28 = 52 cm²

➖ SUBTRACT — "Cutout"

  • Big rect: 12 × 8 = 96 cm²
  • Cut-out: ½ × 10 × 5 = 25 cm²
  • Shaded: 96 − 25 = 71 cm²
