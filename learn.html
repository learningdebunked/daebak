Understanding WHY Formulas Work

Don't just memorize — understand! See why the area formulas make sense.

▭ Rectangle: A = length × width

Why do we multiply? Let's count the squares!

Interactive: Click to change the rectangle size
3 rows × 4 columns = 12 squares
💡 The Big Idea

When you have a rectangle, you're arranging unit squares (1×1 squares) in rows and columns.

Length tells you how many squares fit across (columns).
Width tells you how many rows you have.

So length × width = total number of squares = area!

Example: A 5m × 3m garden

  • You can fit 5 squares across (length)
  • You have 3 rows of squares (width)
  • Total squares: 5 + 5 + 5 = 15, or simply 5 × 3 = 15 m²
A = length × width

△ Triangle: A = ½ × base × height

A triangle is exactly HALF of a rectangle!

Watch: A triangle is half a rectangle
💡 The Big Idea

Imagine a rectangle with the same base and height as your triangle.

If you draw a diagonal line, you split the rectangle into two identical triangles.

So: Triangle area = ½ of rectangle area

Rectangle area = base × height
Triangle area = ½ × base × height

Why we ALWAYS divide by 2

Rectangle

60 cm²

10 cm × 6 cm = 60

Triangle (same base & height)

30 cm²

½ × 10 cm × 6 cm = 30

A = ½ × base × height

▱ Parallelogram: A = base × height

It's a "pushed" rectangle — same area!

Visual Proof: Cut and rearrange
💡 The Big Idea

If you cut a triangle from one side of a parallelogram and move it to the other side, you get a rectangle!

The rectangle has the same base and height as the parallelogram.

So they have the same area: base × height

⚠️ Common Mistake: Don't use the slanted side!

The slanted side is NOT the height. Always use the perpendicular distance (the dotted line with the right-angle square □).

A = base × height

⏢ Trapezoid: A = ½ × (b₁ + b₂) × height

Average the two bases, then treat it like a rectangle!

Think of it as an "average rectangle"
💡 The Big Idea

A trapezoid has two different bases — one short (b₁) and one long (b₂).

Step 1: Find the average width: (b₁ + b₂) ÷ 2

Step 2: Multiply by height, just like a rectangle!

Formula: ½ × (b₁ + b₂) × h

Example: Trapezoid with bases 6 ft and 10 ft, height 4 ft

  • Step 1: Add the bases: 6 + 10 = 16 ft
  • Step 2: Multiply by height: 16 × 4 = 64
  • Step 3: Divide by 2: 64 ÷ 2 = 32 ft²
A = ½ × (b₁ + b₂) × h
