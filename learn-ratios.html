🔢 Learn Ratios

Understand what ratios really mean from first principles. Not just "two numbers with a colon" - but relationships that stay constant!

🍓 What IS a Ratio?

Let's start with something concrete: making fruit bowls!

Imagine you're making fruit bowls for a party. Your recipe says:

Strawberries:
🍓 🍓 🍓 🍓 🍓 🍓 🍓 🍓
Raspberries:
🫐 🫐 🫐 🫐 🫐 🫐 🫐 🫐 🫐 🫐 🫐 🫐
This creates a ratio:
8 : 12
"For every 8 strawberries, we use 12 raspberries"
🤔 Think About It:
  • A ratio is a comparison between two quantities
  • It shows a relationship - not just random numbers
  • Order matters! 8:12 is different from 12:8
  • The ratio tells us the "recipe" for our fruit bowl

Three Ways to Write Ratios

You can write the same ratio in three different ways:

All three mean the same thing!

8 : 12 With a colon
8 to 12 With the word "to"
8/12 As a fraction
💡 Key Understanding

A ratio is NOT just two numbers. It's a relationship that describes how quantities compare to each other. When we say 8:12, we're describing a pattern that we can scale up or down while keeping the same "taste" of our fruit bowl!

📊 Equivalent Ratios

The relationship stays the same when you scale!

What if we need to make more fruit bowls? Let's see what happens when we double, triple, or quadruple our recipe:

Bowls Strawberries Raspberries Ratio Simplified
1 4 6 4:6 2:3
2 8 12 8:12 2:3
3 12 18 12:18 2:3
4 16 24 16:24 2:3
5 20 30 20:30 2:3
🤔 What Do You Notice?
  • When we double the bowls, we double BOTH ingredients
  • The numbers change, but the relationship stays the same
  • All these ratios simplify to 2:3
  • These are called equivalent ratios
💡 Key Understanding

Equivalent ratios are different ways of expressing the same relationship. Just like ½ = 2/4 = 3/6 in fractions, the ratios 2:3 = 4:6 = 8:12 all describe the same "recipe." The fruit bowl tastes the same whether you make 1 or 100!

Pattern: To create equivalent ratios, multiply (or divide) both numbers by the same amount.

2:3 = (2×4):(3×4) = 8:12

🎯 Simplifying Ratios

Find the smallest numbers that show the same relationship

Just like reducing fractions, we can simplify ratios to make them easier to understand. Let's simplify 8:12 step by step:

8 : 12
Original ratio
↓ (divide both by 2)
4 : 6
8÷2 = 4 and 12÷2 = 6
↓ (divide both by 2 again)
2 : 3
4÷2 = 2 and 6÷2 = 3 — Simplest form! ✓

How to Simplify Any Ratio

📝 Step-by-Step Process:
  • Step 1: Find a common factor of both numbers
  • Step 2: Divide BOTH numbers by that factor
  • Step 3: Repeat until you can't divide evenly anymore
  • Step 4: You've found the simplest form!
💡 Why Simplify?

Simplifying makes the relationship easier to see. "2:3" is much clearer than "8:12" - it immediately tells you "for every 2 strawberries, use 3 raspberries." Same relationship, simpler numbers!

Think of it like this: Would you rather follow a recipe that says "2 cups flour to 3 cups water" or "8 cups flour to 12 cups water"? Same ratio, but 2:3 is easier to remember and work with!

⚖️ Scaling Ratios Up and Down

The power of ratios - multiply or divide to scale!

Once you know the simplest form of a ratio, you can easily scale it to any size. Let's say our simplest ratio is 2:3 (strawberries to raspberries).

Scaling UP (Making More)

Need to make 10 bowls? Multiply both sides by 5:

2:3 → (2×5):(3×5) = 10:15

You need 10 strawberries and 15 raspberries

Scaling DOWN (Making Less)

Have a ratio of 10:15 but only need half? Divide both sides by 5:

10:15 → (10÷5):(15÷5) = 2:3

Back to our simplest form!

Scaling Examples

Original Ratio Operation New Ratio Meaning
2:3 ×2 4:6 Double the recipe
2:3 ×3 6:9 Triple the recipe
2:3 ×10 20:30 Make 10 times as much
20:30 ÷10 2:3 Scale back down
💡 The Golden Rule

Always multiply or divide BOTH sides by the same number. This keeps the relationship constant. Think of it like a balanced scale - whatever you do to one side, you must do to the other to keep it balanced!

⚠️ Common Mistakes to Avoid

Learn what NOT to do!

❌ DANGER: Don't Add or Subtract!

The most common mistake is thinking you can add or subtract the same number to both sides of a ratio. This BREAKS the relationship!

❌ WRONG
2:3
↓ add 5 to both
7:8
This is NOT equivalent!
✅ CORRECT
2:3
↓ multiply both by 5
10:15
This IS equivalent!

Why does adding break it? Let's see with our fruit bowls:

  • Original: 2 strawberries for every 3 raspberries (ratio 2:3)
  • If we add 5 to both: 7 strawberries for every 8 raspberries (ratio 7:8)
  • These taste different! 2:3 ≈ 0.67, but 7:8 ≈ 0.875

Other Common Mistakes:

⚠️ Watch Out For:
  • Mixing up the order: 2:3 is NOT the same as 3:2
  • Forgetting units: Always know what you're comparing
  • Not simplifying: 8:12 works, but 2:3 is clearer
  • Dividing when you can't: 5:7 is already simplest (no common factors)
💡 Remember

Ratios show multiplicative relationships, not additive ones. When you double a recipe, you multiply everything by 2. You don't add 2 to everything!

Think: If a recipe calls for 2 cups of flour and you want to double it, you use 4 cups (2×2), not 4 cups (2+2). The same logic applies to ratios!

📚 Summary: What You've Learned

✓ Key Takeaways:
  • Ratios are relationships between quantities, not just numbers
  • Order matters: 2:3 is different from 3:2
  • Three ways to write: 2:3, 2 to 3, or 2/3
  • Equivalent ratios: Same relationship, different numbers (like 2:3 = 4:6 = 8:12)
  • Simplify by dividing: Find common factors and divide both sides
  • Scale by multiplying: Multiply or divide both sides by the same number
  • Never add or subtract: This breaks the relationship!
